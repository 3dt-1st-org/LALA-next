The docent player — flagship screen

The screen the whole product is for

One tap from any card, and it stands on its own rather than living inside a place sheet. Everything here exists to make the generation visible: it announces when it was written, what it was written for, and breaks itself into chapters no human editor produced.

1

“Auto-generated · just now” sits on the artwork. A traveller should never mistake this for a pre-recorded track someone uploaded in 2019.

2

“Written for right now — 25.2°C, clear.” Your live build already writes weather into the script — the current docent text references today’s conditions. Surfacing that makes the freshness undeniable.

3

Auto-chapters prove the generation. Three headings drawn from the place itself do more to demonstrate the AI than any badge reading “AI-powered” ever would.

4

Speech is already built and sitting disabled in /readyz. This screen is a config flip plus a player, not new infrastructure.

A day is a playlist with walking in between

“Play the whole day · 7:24” turns four separate places into a single continuous piece of audio, sequenced in walking order. The transit hops on the rail are the connective tissue the live build omits entirely.

1

One control plays the entire itinerary, auto-advancing between stops. This is the feature that makes an auto-generated docent feel like a tour rather than a lookup.

2

Every stop carries its runtime and its local share, colour-coded by category — enough to judge the shape of a day at a glance.

Build order

Where a small team starts

1

Turn speech on, ship the player

Flip the disabled Speech config and build the full-screen player with the auto-generated badge and chapters. The feature already exists — it just has no surface worth opening.

2

Play control everywhere, mini-player docked

Cards, rows, plan stops, and a persistent bar above the tab bar. Audio stops being a destination and becomes the app’s default state.

3

Finish English mode

Discover and Plan are still fully Korean in English mode, and the tab bar never translates. Mostly string-table work, and nothing else matters to a traveller who can’t read the screen.
